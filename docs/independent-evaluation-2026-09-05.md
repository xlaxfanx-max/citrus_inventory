# Independent system and business evaluation — 5 September 2026

**Verdict: pursue a bounded customer-validation pilot. The repository provides a credible foundation for testing lemon storage decisions; predictive accuracy, operational fit, willingness to pay, and repeatable sales remain unproven.** A profitable specialist business is plausible. There is insufficient evidence to project a large software business or recommend broad deployment.

This assessment independently inspected the implementation, ran its tests, checked local data coverage, reproduced selected model behaviors, and consulted primary public sources. Existing project plans were treated as proposals, not evidence of achieved results. No customer interviews, plant visit, real-phone field trial, production infrastructure test, or customer financial review was performed. Application code was not changed.

**What the system actually does.** The application maintains a lot-level storage record, captures repeated fruit samples, measures peel color from reference-board photographs, forecasts a color threshold, ranks packing priorities, and records management responses and packout outcomes. Its strongest potential product description is: “Help storage managers identify which lemon lots need attention and choose feasible packing actions that preserve fresh-market value.”

This scope matters. If Saticoy's main problem is inaccurate counts, finding bins, tracking pallets, reconciling transfers, or matching finished goods to orders, the current application does not adequately solve that problem. If the problem is discovering deteriorating lots too late or coordinating storage condition with packing decisions, it is much closer. Customer discovery must choose between these problems before further substantial development.

**Why Saticoy is a sensible first customer.** Saticoy describes three packinghouses and annual handling of 9.5 million cartons, of which 6 million go to fresh market and 3.5 million to other products. These are its published company figures, not audited current-year results. Its scale makes even a modest improvement worth investigating. Personal connections can provide access to actual decisions and their consequences. [Saticoy company description](https://saticoylemon.com/about-us/)

Its published workflow includes sorting before storage, storage conditioning, and another grading pass before packing to customer orders. That creates a plausible need for visibility between the two grading events. It does not establish that Saticoy currently lacks that visibility or makes poor decisions. [Saticoy operations](https://saticoylemon.com/operations/)

Saticoy is also a demanding first customer: established operations, existing systems, and multiple stakeholders. A friendly contact is valuable access, but commercial validation requires a person with budget authority and an operating owner willing to allocate staff time. Three plants within Saticoy would demonstrate expansion within one customer; a separate operator is needed to demonstrate repeatable sales.

**The central scientific and product risk.** The current model estimates when mean peel color crosses a threshold, then subtracts a fixed buffer to label a pack-by date. That does not establish the date on which delaying a lot destroys economic value. UC Davis identifies multiple lemon quality dimensions, including color, firmness, decay and physical defects, and describes storage life as dependent on harvest condition and storage circumstances. Its fact sheet also notes that ethylene treatment can accelerate deterioration. Color is a useful signal, but cannot be assumed to represent the whole quality trajectory. [UC Davis lemon fact sheet](https://postharvest.ucdavis.edu/produce-facts-sheets/lemon)

The repository does record additional quality, intake, environment and treatment fields, which is useful. Those variables are currently stored as candidate features rather than used to determine the operational forecast. A more complex model should follow evidence that these observations improve actual decisions. The first useful product might be a shared quality history and exception list, even if precise remaining-life prediction proves unreliable.

**What the implementation gets right.** Imports support row errors and repeat uploads; partial packouts preserve open inventory; plant access is enforced; photos require authorization; failed scoring has a retry/retake path; forecasts retain input provenance; published plans preserve recommendation fields and management decisions. The validation page explicitly distinguishes schedule agreement from biological accuracy. These are substantive foundations for learning from a pilot.

The simple architecture is a reasonable fit for early deployment. Nothing reviewed justifies a rewrite, microservices, or a broad ERP expansion. Production configuration guards, a container, CI and a recovery runbook exist. They are preparation for operations, not evidence that a real deployment has survived outages, restored backups, or handled production load. The existing CI tests run without a Postgres service; production-database behavior and concurrent jobs still need representative staging verification.

These are bounded issues to fix and verify; they do not imply that the whole system is unsalvageable. Passing tests do not contradict these findings: tests can confirm that a specified behavior executes correctly while leaving its business meaning untested.

**Integration assumptions to settle using real records.** The current model assigns one current room to a lot and allows one packout record per lot per day. Duplicate same-day rows within an upload are rejected; a later upload updates that day's existing record. That is workable if the source contract supplies complete daily aggregates. It is not suitable for blindly importing individual packing runs. Likewise, if one source lot is divided across storage rooms or becomes multiple storage cohorts after grading, one room and one color trajectory may be misleading. Trace ten actual lots through intake, sorting, storage, partial packing and final closure before treating the schema as operationally sufficient. [Lot and packout models](<C:/Users/Owner/Desktop/Citrus Inventory/lots/models.py>), [packout importer](<C:/Users/Owner/Desktop/Citrus Inventory/lots/importers/packout.py>)

CSV is an acceptable pilot interface if an owner reconciles it on a defined schedule. A template upload is not yet a supported Famous integration. Confirm actual identifiers, reused lot numbers, quantity units, partials, corrections, transfers and source freshness. Suppress unwarranted recommendations when the inventory record is stale or uncertain. A live database readiness check cannot detect a stopped scoring job or a failed daily import.

**Sampling may determine viability more than image processing.** The ten-fruit workflow is useful for exploring color, but weak for detecting uncommon defects. Under ideal independent random sampling, a lot with 2% defective fruit has an 81.7% probability of showing zero defects in ten fruit: `0.98^10`. Even twenty independently sampled fruit miss it 66.8% of the time. Real bin clustering can make simple sampling assumptions inappropriate. A zero count must not be read as a clean lot.

The UI's “about one minute” is a capture aspiration, not a verified total labor estimate. Staff must find the lot, access representative bins, retrieve fruit, walk to the station, photograph, inspect, retake when needed, and handle the sample afterward. Illustratively, 50 lots at 6–10 minutes each consume 5–8.3 staff-hours per weekly round before holdouts and administration. Time the entire route. Repeatedly collecting the easiest visible fruit could produce clean charts with poor decisions.

**Economic test.** Total products volume is not recoverable waste. Some downgrades may arise before storage, reflect grade or size, or be commercially rational. Better timing has value only where there is an alternative feasible action and demand for the resulting product. For a cooperative, determine whether recovered value accrues to growers, the packing operation, or both, and which budget can pay for the tool.

Use finance-approved comparable units and net returns:

`Annual customer net benefit = attributable recovered fresh cartons × incremental net value per carton + verified operating savings − added operating cost − software fees`

Incremental net value must account for the displaced products return and additional packing, handling and other variable costs. Avoid counting the same benefit both as higher yield and avoided loss.

The following are sensitivity examples, not estimates of Saticoy results, vendor prices, or validated willingness to pay. Assume $4 incremental net value per recovered carton, $10,000 annual additional operating cost, a $15,000 annual software fee, no other savings, and exclude one-time setup:

| Attributable recovered cartons/year | Gross incremental value | Customer annual net benefit |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,500 | $10,000 | −$15,000 |
| 10,000 | $40,000 | $15,000 |
| 25,000 | $100,000 | $75,000 |

Break-even in that example is 6,250 recovered cartons annually; first-year setup raises it. A small percentage applied to company-wide volume can look attractive while overstating the volume a one-plant pilot actually influences. Estimate benefits from the eligible lots and the decisions the software changes.

For the supplier, include onboarding, travel, calibration equipment, replacements, training, data reconciliation, hosting and ongoing support. If every plant requires persistent founder analysis and bespoke exports, the initial business behaves like a specialist service. That can be worthwhile, but should be priced and staffed accordingly. A fixed-scope paid pilot followed by a site subscription and a defined implementation fee is a reasonable commercial hypothesis. Avoid promising a savings-based fee before both sides accept attribution.

**The validation sequence I would fund.**

1. **Establish the problem before expanding the product.** During the next two weeks, interview the plant/storage manager, an experienced foreman, QC, finance and the data-system owner. Reconstruct ten recent problem lots and several comparable successful lots. Identify what was known at the time, which decision could realistically have changed, and the financial consequence. Obtain representative exports and inspect the existing Clarifruit workflow. Interview two other operators early to expose Saticoy-specific assumptions.
2. **Qualify a real pilot.** Require a named sponsor with budget authority, an operating owner, scheduled staff time, data access and a written conversion decision. Prefer a paid pilot. If initial discovery is unpaid, make it short and bounded. Friendly feedback and open-ended free trials are weak demand evidence.
3. **Prove observation and workflow in shadow mode.** Start at one plant with roughly 30–50 manageable lots, resolve the findings above, and reconcile physical cohorts with source records. Compare phone measurements against an independent instrument and repeated operator assessments. Track total staff time, successful sampling, freshness and outcome completeness. Collect human decisions before revealing recommendations.
4. **Test incremental decision value prospectively.** Use contemporaneous comparable lots or appropriately assigned packing blocks, with operations retaining authority and existing QC protections. Compare the existing process, a simple age/current-condition rule, and the proposed model at the same decision horizons. Account for shared packing capacity: moving one lot forward can delay another, so measure whole-plan consequences. Do not infer causality from a simple on-time-versus-late chart.
5. **Make an explicit commercial decision.** Continue only if managers act on useful information, the full workflow cost is acceptable, finance accepts a conservative positive benefit estimate, and someone commits budget to continued use. If observations are valuable but the model adds little, simplify toward shared quality history and exception management. If loss is mostly unavoidable, sampling cannot represent the fruit economically, or useful recommendations cannot be executed, stop or change the proposition.

An 8–12 week initial pilot may establish feasibility but may not observe enough storage failures or seasonal variation to establish annual ROI. Twenty final packouts is an initial benchmark check, not statistical proof of small improvements. Size a later outcome evaluation using measured lot-to-lot variation and the minimum benefit that justifies the cost. Holdout fruit also needs its own cohort identity and handling/exposure record; its failure does not automatically equal whole-lot failure. Freeze the analysis rules before evaluating outcomes, keep entire lots within a validation split, and audit historical feature availability before using the training export for claims about prospective performance.

**What would change the verdict.** Positive evidence would be a manager showing repeated costly, preventable storage decisions; a workable data and sampling routine; recommendations that improve those decisions over a fair baseline; and a paid continuation. Negative evidence would be an existing tool already meeting the need, no actionable advance warning, benefits smaller than added labor/support, or continued use depending on your personal presence. The next milestone should be proof of a paid operational improvement at one plant. That is the most valuable uncertainty to resolve now.
