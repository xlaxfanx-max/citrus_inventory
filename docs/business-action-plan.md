# Citrus storage decision support: action plan

**Status:** Draft for review  
**Prepared:** 2 September 2026  
**Initial deployment:** One Saticoy Lemon Association plant  
**Planning horizon:** Pilot through 12-month product decision

## 1. Decision requested

Approve a controlled, one-plant pilot of the citrus storage decision-support system. The pilot should test whether repeated lot-level measurements and a ranked pack plan can prevent avoidable loss of fresh-market value without disrupting plant operations.

Do not yet approve a broad rollout, a new machine-learning model, or expansion into a general ERP or quality-control platform. Those decisions should follow evidence from the pilot gates in this plan.

## 2. Product proposition

The product is a **citrus storage decision layer** that sits on top of Famous and existing plant systems. It identifies which lots are most likely to lose marketable value first and turns that risk into a practical weekly pack plan.

The customer outcome is not “better inventory visibility” or “AI photo analysis.” The intended outcomes are:

- More fresh-market cartons from fruit already received.
- Fewer lots packed late because biological condition was not visible.
- Fewer emergency packs, avoidable changeovers, and overloaded weeks.
- A consistent, auditable decision process across plants and foremen.
- A proprietary longitudinal dataset connecting intake, storage, treatment, observed quality, decisions, and final packout.

The system should complement—not replace—Famous, existing room sensors, laboratory measurements, or line graders.

## 3. Business rationale

Saticoy reports annual handling of approximately 9.5 million cartons, including 6 million fresh-market cartons and 3.5 million used in other products. At that volume, small improvements can be meaningful:

| Improvement across handled volume | Carton-equivalents affected |
|---:|---:|
| 0.10% | 9,500 |
| 0.25% | 23,750 |
| 0.50% | 47,500 |
| 1.00% | 95,000 |

These figures are sensitivity examples, not a claim that all products volume is recoverable. The pilot must identify the narrower share of downgrades caused by avoidable storage timing.

The financial calculation should use Saticoy's actual realized economics from Famous:

```text
Incremental value =
    recovered fresh cartons
    × (net fresh return − net products return − incremental handling cost)
  + avoided emergency labor, changeovers, and customer claims
  − sampling, hardware, software, and operating cost
```

The current “fresh is roughly ten times products” assumption must not be used as the investment case until finance validates it using comparable units and net returns.

## 4. Current readiness

### What is already built

- Three-plant roles and access controls.
- Receiving, packout, room-move, room-condition, and treatment imports.
- Mobile-first sample capture with private photo storage.
- Reference-board detection, color correction, per-fruit CCI, and quality checks.
- Lot-level color trajectories and pack-by estimates.
- Ranked packing plan, attention filters, and eight-week capacity outlook.
- Monday email report and report-delivery audit.
- Packout, direct-quality, representative-sample, and shelf-life-holdout fields.
- Leakage-safe training export and model provenance.
- Production container, deployment checks, health endpoints, and backup/rollback guidance.
- Seventy-nine passing automated tests as of this draft.

### What is not yet proven

- The current database is demo data generated by the local seed command. It is not evidence of field accuracy or business value.
- The phone/board measurement has not been validated against a colorimeter under plant conditions.
- The current model predicts time to a peel-color threshold, not remaining marketable life.
- A ten-fruit sample is not a precise estimate of low-percent decay or other rare defects.
- There are currently no live room-condition or treatment records in the local dataset.
- CSV exchange is not yet an operational integration with Famous.
- The application does not record whether management accepted, deferred, or overrode each recommendation.
- The ranked list does not yet incorporate customer commitments, size/grade demand, line availability, or changeover cost.

## 5. Scope

### In scope for the pilot

- One plant and 30–50 active lots.
- Weekly representative sampling and same-visit photo retakes.
- Direct packout-quality labels and structured downgrade reasons.
- A limited number of representative shelf-life holdouts.
- Foreman estimates retained as the independent benchmark.
- A weekly recommendation that remains advisory to the GM.
- Prospective measurement of adoption, operational impact, and economic outcome.
- Automation of the minimum Famous data exchange required for reliable daily operation.

### Out of scope until a later gate

- Replacing Famous inventory, accounting, traceability, recall, WMS, or sales functions.
- A generic configurable quality-control product.
- Proprietary sensors, spectroscopy, or inline grading hardware.
- Broad defect-recognition computer vision.
- Automatic execution of the packing schedule.
- Oranges, mandarins, other crops, multi-tenancy, and external billing.
- Promotion of a complex model before real holdout labels exist.

## 6. Phased action plan

### Phase 0 — authorize and prepare

**Timing:** Weeks 0–2  
**Objective:** Remove organizational, measurement, and data-access ambiguity before collecting pilot evidence.

| ID | Action | Suggested owner | Deliverable / acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | Name the executive sponsor, operating owner, pilot foreman, QC lead, IT contact, and engineering owner. | Saticoy leadership | Named people with allocated weekly time and escalation path. |
| 0.2 | Select the pilot plant and confirm the live dates, active rooms, expected lot count, and sampling day. | GM / operations | Written pilot scope covering 30–50 lots. |
| 0.3 | Define “meets pack specification,” biological failure, and the allowed downgrade-reason taxonomy. | QC lead | Approved written definitions used in capture and packout files. |
| 0.4 | Extract one representative Famous receiving, room-move, inventory, and packout file. | IT / Famous owner | Files map successfully into the application without manual reinterpretation. |
| 0.5 | Establish the economic baseline: net fresh and products returns, handling cost, emergency labor, and relevant claims/rejections. | Finance / GM | Signed-off ROI input sheet with units and data sources. |
| 0.6 | Print and permanently identify the pilot board; install the fixed light and phone mount. | Plant operations | Station installed to the hardware specification. |
| 0.7 | Measure board patches and paired fruit with a calibrated colorimeter or spectrophotometer. | QC lead | Reference values recorded; repeatability tolerance agreed before evaluation. |
| 0.8 | Confirm Wi-Fi/cellular coverage and same-visit scoring at the station. | IT / engineering | Successful end-to-end uploads and results from the plant location. |
| 0.9 | Deploy the production environment, private storage, email, monitoring, backups, and restore test. | Engineering / IT | Staging acceptance and documented recovery test completed. |
| 0.10 | Freeze the pilot protocol and analysis plan before seeing outcome results. | Sponsor / analyst | Versioned protocol identifying calibration and holdout lots. |

**Exit gate:** Do not begin evaluation sampling until measurement references, outcome definitions, data access, and operational ownership are approved.

### Phase 1 — run the field pilot

**Timing:** Weeks 3–10  
**Objective:** Prove that the measurement and sampling workflow is reliable enough to inform decisions.

| ID | Action | Suggested owner | Deliverable / acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Train samplers on across-bin random selection, photo setup, defects, retakes, and holdouts. | QC lead | Observed competency check completed for every sampler. |
| 1.2 | Sample the pilot lots weekly at approximately the same day and time. | Foreman / sampler | At least 90% of in-scope lots sampled on schedule or assigned a documented exception. |
| 1.3 | Retake failed or low-quality photos during the same visit. | Foreman / sampler | At least 95% of visits end with a usable photo; causes of failure are categorized. |
| 1.4 | Import actual room moves, raw condition readings, harvest dates, and treatment events where available. | IT / QC | Weekly coverage report reviewed; missing data is not interpreted as zero exposure. |
| 1.5 | Record every partial and final packout with direct observed quality and structured downgrade reasons. | QC / packout team | Complete outcome record for every evaluated final packout. |
| 1.6 | Maintain selected fixed holdout groups beyond the commercial pack date. | QC lead | First observed pass-to-fail interval and limiting failure reason captured. |
| 1.7 | Publish the advisory recommendation and review it with the GM each week. | Product owner / GM | Recommendation review occurs before the weekly schedule is locked. |
| 1.8 | Record whether every recommendation was accepted, deferred, or overridden and why. | GM / engineering | Decision record linked to the plan version and eventual outcome. |
| 1.9 | Review data-quality and safety exceptions weekly without retuning evaluation thresholds. | Pilot working group | Signed weekly exception log and corrective actions. |

**Required product work during Phase 1:**

1. Recommendation-plan versioning and decision history.
2. Accepted/deferred/overridden status with structured reason and notes.
3. Board, phone, light, and calibration identifiers on each photo.
4. Data-freshness and missing-import warnings.
5. Offline upload queue with visible retry status, or a documented and tested equivalent if network coverage is fully reliable.
6. Barcode or QR lot lookup if manual lot selection causes measurable delay or mistakes.

**Minimum evidence gate:**

- At least 30 lots with two or more usable CCI samples.
- At least 20 final packouts with direct quality labels for a basic benchmark.
- Repeatability and colorimeter agreement reviewed by the QC lead.
- Sampling and photo workflow meets the operational metrics below.
- Important errors reviewed by plant, room, variety, sampler, phone, and board.

This is a minimum gate for interpretation, not sufficient evidence for a multivariable remaining-life model.

### Phase 2 — integrate the workflow and measure decisions

**Timing:** Weeks 8–16, overlapping the end of Phase 1  
**Objective:** Remove duplicate entry and turn the recommendation into an auditable management process.

| ID | Action | Suggested owner | Deliverable / acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Select the supported Famous exchange method: scheduled export/SFTP, API, or local read-only connector. | IT / Famous / engineering | Security and support approach approved. |
| 2.2 | Automate inbound lots, quantities, room moves, status, and packouts. | Engineering | Daily reconciliation completes without manual re-keying; failures alert an owner. |
| 2.3 | Reconcile application inventory against Famous and expose stale or incomplete data. | Engineering / operations | Exceptions visible by lot with an assigned resolution owner. |
| 2.4 | Build the prospective ROI ledger using actual packout and net-value data. | Finance / analyst | Lot-level economic results can be aggregated by week, plant, and decision status. |
| 2.5 | Record schedule locks, customer/grade constraints, and manual commitments. | GM / engineering | The system can explain why biological urgency did or did not control a decision. |

**Exit gate:** The application may be used for broader operational planning only after data freshness is visible, recommendations are attributable to a specific plan, and actual outcomes flow back automatically or through a controlled reconciliation.

### Phase 3 — build a feasible planning recommendation

**Timing:** Months 4–8  
**Objective:** Move from a biologically ranked list to a plan that operations can execute.

Add planning inputs in this order:

1. Daily and weekly packing capacity.
2. Locked customer orders and ship dates.
3. Required size, grade, label, and destination.
4. Expected grade yield and available bins by lot.
5. Partial-pack rules.
6. Line availability, run minimums, and changeover cost.
7. Manual locks and safety overrides.

The recommendation should show:

- The proposed lot sequence and quantities.
- The biological and commercial reason for each priority.
- The consequence of delay, including risk and value at stake.
- Unfilled demand and capacity overloads.
- Which constraints forced a lower-risk lot ahead of a higher-risk lot.
- A comparison with the actual management plan.

Run the constrained recommendation in shadow mode before using it to set the schedule.

### Phase 4 — earn and validate remaining-marketable-life prediction

**Timing:** Months 5–10, dependent on label coverage  
**Objective:** Predict biological failure with calibrated uncertainty instead of treating peel color as the complete shelf-life signal.

Maintain two related outputs:

- **Color readiness:** when the lot is expected to reach the target peel-color stage.
- **Remaining marketable life:** time to first failure of the written pack specification, with the limiting reason.

Candidate features may include intake CCI, fruit-level CCI distribution, longitudinal color, variety, grower/block, harvest-to-receive time, room exposure, temperature, humidity, gas readings, treatments, and current observed defects.

The remaining-life model should support right-censored holdouts, competing failure reasons, and P10/P50/P90 dates. Validation must split entire lots and reserve a future time or season block for final testing.

**Promotion gate:** Keep the current linear benchmark in production until the challenger:

- Improves fixed-horizon results at 7, 14, and 28 days on unseen lots.
- Improves the simulated packing decision, not only statistical error.
- Produces acceptably calibrated uncertainty intervals.
- Meets agreed early-failure recall and false-alert limits.
- Shows no hidden safety failure in an important plant, room, variety, or data-coverage subgroup.
- Runs in shadow mode successfully and has a tested rollback path.

### Phase 5 — rollout and productization decision

**Timing:** Months 9–12  
**Objective:** Decide whether to scale within Saticoy and whether a repeatable commercial product exists.

| Decision | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Roll out to all Saticoy plants | Operational reliability, positive net benefit, management adoption, and successful data integration at the pilot plant. |
| Sell to a second citrus operator | Repeatable hardware/SOP package, configurable site definitions, secure tenant isolation, support model, and a connector strategy. |
| Partner with Famous | Evidence that the product creates value without duplicating Famous; a supported integration and joint customer case. |
| Integrate with Clarifresh or AgroFresh | Customer already uses the system or its data materially improves the decision recommendation. |
| Expand to another commodity | A named customer problem, relevant biological signal, labeling plan, and commodity-specific validation budget. |

Commercial packaging should begin with a paid pilot credited toward an annual per-plant subscription. A performance fee should be considered only if the attribution method is accepted by both parties.

## 7. Pilot scorecard

Targets marked “set before pilot” require sponsor approval before live evaluation begins.

| Category | Metric | Initial target |
|---|---|---:|
| Workflow | Median capture time per lot | 90 seconds or less |
| Workflow | Visits ending with a usable photo | 95% or more |
| Workflow | Photo scored after upload | Within 2 minutes |
| Coverage | In-scope lots sampled on schedule or excepted | 90% or more |
| Data | Packouts with direct quality labels | 95% or more |
| Data | Recommendations with a recorded management decision | 95% or more |
| Data | Operational-source freshness | Less than 24 hours, unless approved otherwise |
| Adoption | Recommendations acknowledged before schedule lock | Set before pilot |
| Adoption | Override reasons completed | 95% or more |
| Outcome | Avoidable late biological failures | Baseline and target set before pilot |
| Outcome | Emergency packs and avoidable changeovers | Baseline and target set before pilot |
| Outcome | Incremental fresh yield attributable to timing | Positive net result with sensitivity analysis |
| Model | Early-failure recall / false-alert rate | Safety limits set before model evaluation |
| Model | P10–P90 interval coverage | Calibration target set before V2 promotion |

Capture both the central result and a conservative sensitivity range. Do not declare success from schedule-date mean absolute error alone: management chooses pack dates, so they are not biological truth.

## 8. Governance and review cadence

### Weekly pilot working group

Participants: operating owner, pilot foreman, QC lead, IT/data owner, and engineering.

Agenda:

1. Sampling, photo, import, and condition-data coverage.
2. Failed measurements and same-visit retakes.
3. New quality risks and safety overrides.
4. Recommendation decisions and override reasons.
5. Packouts, holdout failures, and missing labels.
6. Corrective actions with named owners and dates.

### Monthly steering review

Participants: executive sponsor, GM, finance, QC, IT, and product/engineering.

Review:

- Operational scorecard.
- Outcome and ROI ledger.
- Adoption and override patterns.
- Data integrity and security.
- Scope, staffing, and schedule.
- Continue, modify, pause, or stop decision.

Thresholds and model rules must be versioned. Evaluation thresholds may not be retuned on the same lots used to report performance.

## 9. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Consequence | Mitigation / decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Peel color is treated as complete shelf life | Apparently accurate color dates miss decay, shrivel, softness, or chilling injury. | Maintain separate color-readiness and quality-failure outcomes; do not market v1 as ripeness prediction. |
| Sampling is biased toward visible or convenient fruit | Lot condition appears better or worse than reality. | Across-bin protocol, recorded selection method, training, and exclusion/sensitivity analysis for convenience samples. |
| Board, phone, or lighting drift | False changes in CCI create incorrect urgency. | Permanent IDs, patch references, repeatability checks, cleaning, controlled lighting, and recalibration triggers. |
| Ten fruit is interpreted as a precise defect rate | Low-percent risks are over- or understated. | Describe counts as sample observations; use the site's larger QC sample where statistical inference is required. |
| Manual CSV work becomes routine | Stale data and duplicate effort destroy trust. | Time-box CSVs to the pilot and automate the supported Famous exchange in Phase 2. |
| Good recommendations are ignored or bad ones followed blindly | Value cannot be attributed and operations may be harmed. | Advisory status, acknowledgments, structured overrides, plan history, and GM accountability. |
| Seasonal variation is mistaken for product impact | ROI claim is not credible. | Prospective baseline, contemporaneous comparison where possible, lot-level decision history, and conservative sensitivity analysis. |
| Complex ML is introduced too early | Overfitting and false confidence. | Maintain the auditable linear benchmark until the promotion gate is passed. |
| Product expands into ERP or generic QC | Slow delivery and direct competition with entrenched systems. | Enforce the non-goals and integrate with established systems. |
| Sensitive operational data is exposed or lost | Commercial, trust, and continuity damage. | Private storage, least-privilege access, backup/restore testing, retention rules, and audit logs. |

## 10. Review questions and decisions to complete

The reviewer should answer or assign each item:

1. Is “protect fresh-market value through better pack timing” the correct primary objective?
2. Which plant should run the first pilot, and on what dates?
3. Who owns the operating decision and who may override a recommendation?
4. What written specification determines whether a holdout or packed lot is marketable?
5. Which downgrade reasons are actually available and reliable today?
6. Which Famous data exchange is supported by Saticoy IT and Famous?
7. What are the comparable net fresh and products values by carton or equivalent unit?
8. What baseline period and comparison design will finance accept for attribution?
9. What early-failure and false-alert limits are operationally acceptable?
10. Is offline capture required at the selected station?
11. What staffing is available for weekly sampling, holdouts, QC review, and data reconciliation?
12. Which results would cause the pilot to stop rather than expand?
