Saticoy lemon storage tracker · Pre-launch branch

Product review and outside research, 10 September 2026

Branch prelaunch-items, uncommitted 41 files · +1,218 / −366 133 tests pass Go-live target mid-October 2026

Review of the pre-launch work built on 7 September, a walk-through as GM and as foreman on desktop and phone, an independent code review of the diff, and three outside-research passes on ground the earlier research did not cover: how stored lemons actually fail, how good packinghouses run their rooms and grades, and how a quality-based rotation forecast should be validated.

Verdict

The branch closes seven of the ten 7 September items cleanly and the plumbing is sound: every new endpoint is role-gated and plant-pinned, migrations are additive, the Spanish catalogue is complete. Two things stand between it and launch. The new temperature prior is applied to days already spent, keyed to the lot's current room, so a room move silently rewrites a lot's colour history and can erase its pack date. And the headline number is fragile: on 10 September not one of the 28 demo lots shows a pack date, because a forecast older than a day or a sample older than ten days blanks the date instead of degrading it.

The research changes the science more than the screens. Decay thresholds should follow the USDA grade ladder (1 % at shipping point, 3 % at destination) rather than a single 2 % flag. The storage-temperature band depends on colour class and has a cold-side wall the app does not watch. Lots already yellow need a hold budget, not a pack-by date in the past. And the pilot evaluation must be pre-registered around forecast skill, because a one-to-two-month pilot cannot statistically show a 10 to 20 % cut in dump rate.

Findings from the walk-through

F1No lot on the board has a pack date today

All 28 lots read "Indicative pack date · Not available" and the "Known bins due · 7 days" tile is 0. Two rules suppress the date entirely: a forecast older than one day, and a latest usable sample ten or more days old. Running the nightly build cleared the first; the second still blanks 27 of 28 lots because the demo samples are 9 to 14 days old. In production a missed sampling week erases every forecast the GM plans against. The date should degrade, not vanish: show the last usable date greyed with its sample age, keep the bins-due tile counting it, and reserve "Not available" for lots with no forecast at all.

F2Lots already yellow get a pack-by date in the past and no forward guidance

Lot 26-1021 crossed CCI 2.0 around 22 June. Under model v1 the record said "pack by Aug 25"; under v1.2 it says "pack by June 15", so the same lot went from 16 to 87 days overdue between plan v1 and today with no new evidence. Retaining the crossing is the honest choice, but a date months in the past says nothing about how long the fruit can still be held. The hold-budget ladder above is still not implemented. For lots at or past a band the operative number is days of hold budget remaining, driven by weeks since crossing, decay trend and the storage clock. The same 15 points also went from "Strong trend fit" to "Low trend support" because the staleness rule now forces confidence low: a label change, not a fit change.

F3The board and the plan disagree on open decisions

The board says 7 recommendations await a decision; the plan page says 15. The board counts live rows whose current action requires a decision; the plan counts frozen recommendations. Show the plan's number on the board, since that is what the scorecard measures, and explain any difference.

F4The rot-risk clock sits on a knife edge and prints noise

The clock counts weeks at or above 13.0 °C. The seeded cold rooms run at 55 °F, which is 12.8 °C, so one degree Fahrenheit flips a lot from "0.0 wk" to counting its whole life. Saticoy's own published storage setting is 50 to 52 °F, so the clock as tuned would never trip at the real plant. Today "0.0 wk warm storage" appears on all 28 rows, every phone card and the lot page.

F5The capture screen contradicts itself on the fruit count

Step 1 reads "Fotografíe 10 frutas / Diez limones en el tablero" while step 4 reads "Frutas podridas de 25". The board holds ten lemons and the second photo is optional, so a foreman cannot photograph 25. Say it plainly: colour from the 10 photographed fruit, decay from 25 inspected, or move to three photos. As built, the 25-fruit argument applies only to the decay count.

F6Spanish stops at the foreman's own screens

The foreman's board mixes languages ("Prioridades del inventario" beside "Lot / grower", "Deferred", "16 days overdue"); lot detail and plan detail have no translation tags, and the foreman can open all three.

F7Smaller items

  • Photo cards print "no image file" for every demo photo; show a placeholder.
  • The foreman answered "Yellow · pack within 1 wk" ten weeks running on lot 26-1021 while it stayed in storage. Surface "foreman has said pack-this-week for n weeks" as its own flag; it is the strongest human signal the system collects.
  • Validation reports 0 of 14 packed lots labelled; the new packout quality form is only a collapsed row on the lot page. Link to it from Validation.
  • Readiness shows 0 timestamped room moves and 0 temperature readings, so the temperature-band model runs on setpoints alone.

2 · What the outside research says

Three agents, roughly 100 searches and direct reads of the USDA lemon standard and inspection instructions, UC Davis, CRI South Africa, Saticoy's own operations page, the Sunkist packinghouse licence and about forty journal papers. Every claim is cited in the appendix; blocked sources are flagged there.

2.1 Storage physiology and treatments · agent 1

  • Decay thresholds are regulatory. U.S. No. 1 and No. 2 lemons allow at most 1 % decay at shipping point and 3 % at destination, inside 5 % serious damage and 10 % total defects; decay is serious damage in any amount and contact spot scores the same. The "5 % dump" rule appears only in secondary sources.
  • Sour rot is the California long-storage problem. A 2020 to 2023 packinghouse survey was triggered by high sour rot on propiconazole-treated lemons in extended storage; resistant strains carry no fitness cost, and senescent tissue raised sour rot from 5 % to 68 %. Imazalil, TBZ, pyrimethanil and fludioxonil do not control it; natamycin does. Decay type matters, not just decay count.
  • The temperature window has two walls. Chilling injury below about 10 °C over months and below 5 °C quickly; high rot at 13 °C after three months. Sources disagree on the optimum; the reconciling reading is a colour-class band, greener fruit warmer.
  • Peteca and ethylene. Peteca can appear 3 to 5 days after harvest, yellow fruit is more susceptible than silver, 100 % RH raises it, polyethylene wax more than carnauba. Storage rooms should stay at or below 0.1 ppm ethylene; button senescence is the entry for Alternaria.
  • Weight loss runs about 1.2 % per week at 8 °C and 85 to 90 % RH; softening tracks water loss, which is why storage wax goes on before the hold.
  • Treatment timing and residues. Treat within 24 hours of harvest in warm weather; 2,4-D is prohibited in many export markets; Japan labels postharvest fungicides as food additives; Korea's positive list defaults to 0.01 ppm. Export lots carry more sour-rot exposure because export-friendly chemistry does not control it.

2.2 Packinghouse processes and standards · agent 2

  • No citrus body publishes a rotation rule. The principle is quality-based: green Eureka stores about 90 days at 10 °C, yellow about 30; juice and acid rise during storage, so green fruit gains value for weeks before it loses it. How a house picks the next lot when colour, age, decay and customer spec conflict is tacit knowledge.
  • Saticoy's flow is two-pass. Wash and pre-grade, storage by size and grade at 50 to 52 °F and 90 to 95 % RH, then re-wash, pack wax, second optical grade and hand inspection. The inventory unit is the pre-graded bin: size and colour known, final grade unknown until it runs.
  • Why true FEFO is hard. Bins are stacked several high and the oldest is often at the back or bottom; blocked aisles are a documented decay cause. Bin position is a planning input.
  • Grades encode "ready" and "no longer saleable". The USDA standard, amended 13 May 2026, separates a 10 % colour tolerance, firmness, and the decay ladder; California adds a 15 % aggregate. Export eligibility is set by grove and packinghouse programs, not fruit quality.
  • Traceability. Fresh citrus is not on the FSMA 204 list (compliance date for listed foods is 20 July 2028); PTI GS1-128 labels are the retailer-driven norm.
  • Pool accounting. Under the Sunkist packinghouse licence, growers are paid the pool's average for the grade, size and period in which fruit was packed and sold. The pack date decides the pool, so a hold decision moves grower money, which is the case for the decision record.
  • No public lemon benchmarks exist for repack loss, dump %, days in storage or room fill.

2.3 Rotation science and validation · agent 3

  • FEFO evidence is real but not citrus. Strawberry and banana pilots cut losses by 8 to 14 % of volume; dynamic shelf life extended usable life 13.8 % for fruit. Per-box shelf life carried a ±5-day SD, so lot-average forecasts are the realistic ambition.
  • Kiwifruit is the commercial analogue. Zespri releases lines by the firmness of the third-softest of 90 fruit, sets ship-by weeks by storage characteristic, and pays growers for storage potential. A ship-by week per lot is the hold budget the app is missing.
  • What 25 fruit can say. The lot mean is known to ±0.39 s; with a within-lot SD of 2 CCI that is ±0.8. Percentiles are different: with 25 fruit the second-lowest is about the 8th percentile. The app's P10/P90 from 10 fruit are the minimum and maximum. Report mean ± SE or a tolerance bound.
  • Validation. Repeated samples within a lot are correlated, so random splits inflate skill: use leave-one-lot-out and forward-chaining hindcast, RMSE in days against a naive baseline, and calibration. Lots packed before reaching the band are right-censored; evaluating only lots that reached it is selection bias.
  • Power. Showing a 20 % relative cut in a 20 % lot dump rate needs about 1,447 lots per arm; a 50 % cut still needs 199. A continuous per-lot outcome needs about 44 lots per arm for a 3-point drop with SD 5. Interleave weeks or rooms rather than pre/post.
  • Temperature. ±1 °C is ±7 to 13 % of days; temperature variability tripled prediction uncertainty relative to kinetic parameters; commercial rooms swing about 3 °C daily and run warmer near doors. Logger data beat setpoints.
  • Decision rules and people. Act when P(event) exceeds the cost-loss ratio; uncertainty forecasts improved decisions and preserved trust after misses; 75 to 80 % of system forecasts get manually adjusted, and people use an algorithm more when they can modify it. An "accepted %" target would be the wrong scorecard.

4 · Caveats on the research

  • Several primary PDFs were blocked to automated reading (NSW DPI lemon manual, Argentine protocol, PrimusGFS Module 5, CRI Toolkit 6.4, Zespri SLA, Hertog 2014 full text); facts from them come from abstracts, snippets or secondary citations and are marked in the appendix.
  • No published lot-level decay curve, rotation rule, inspection interval or shrink benchmark exists for commercial lemon storage anywhere; the alarm thresholds above are assembled from grade standards and single studies and should be house-configurable.
  • The USDA lemon standard changed on 13 May 2026; tolerances quoted are from the current text.
  • Sample-size and power figures were computed from standard formulas, not taken from a lemon study.
